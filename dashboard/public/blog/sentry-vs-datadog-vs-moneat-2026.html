Sentry: The Gold Standard (With Caveats)

Sentry built the error monitoring category. Its SDK ecosystem is unmatched — virtually every language and framework is covered, and the developer experience is polished. If you're an individual developer or a small team with straightforward needs, Sentry's $29/month Team plan is genuinely good.

The problems emerge at scale.

Sentry's Pricing Trap

Sentry uses per-category pay-as-you-go budgets. Every data type — errors, replays, spans, logs, profiles — has its own budget and overage rate. Here's what that looks like in practice:

  • Errors: 50,000 included on the Team plan. Overages are ~$0.000188/error at medium volumes.
  • Session replays: 50 included. Overages at $0.00375/replay.
  • Logs: 5 GB included. Overages at $0.50/GB.
  • Uptime monitors: 1 included. Additional monitors at $1/monitor.

For a growing startup burning through 500K errors/month, 200 replays, and 50 GB of logs, the Team plan bill quickly balloons from $29 to ~$145/month — 5× the sticker price.

At 5M errors/month (a common threshold for mid-sized apps), Sentry's Business plan costs over $1,100/month when you account for overages.

What Sentry Is Missing

Despite the feature breadth, Sentry conspicuously lacks several capabilities that modern engineering teams need:

  • No status pages — you need a separate tool (Statuspage.io, BetterStack)
  • No on-call / incident management — you need PagerDuty or OpsGenie
  • No infrastructure monitoring — you need Datadog or Grafana
  • No LLM / AI observability — a major gap as teams deploy AI features
  • Seer AI debugger is $40/active contributor/month extra — a steep add-on

Sentry's AI debugging assistant (Seer) sounds great until you realize it's priced per "active contributor" per month. For a team of 10 active developers, that's $400/month on top of your existing plan.

Datadog: The Enterprise Swiss Army Knife

Datadog is the platform engineering leaders buy when they want to cover everything. And it does cover everything — APM, logs, infrastructure, synthetics, RUM, dashboards, security, CI visibility. The catalog is enormous.

The problem? You pay for every single product separately, and the bills are notoriously difficult to predict.

What Datadog Does Well

To be fair: Datadog's breadth is genuinely impressive. The dashboards are powerful, the APM traces are detailed, and the integrations are extensive. For large enterprises where observability is a core infrastructure investment, Datadog makes sense.

But for startups and mid-market companies? The complexity and cost create real problems.

What Datadog Doesn't Have

  • No self-hosting — your data lives in Datadog's cloud, full stop
  • No Sentry SDK compatibility — you'll rewrite instrumentation to switch
  • No status pages — another tool needed
  • No LLM cost/token analytics — basic AI monitoring only
  • Vendor lock-in — proprietary agents and SDKs make switching painful

What People Get Wrong About Error Monitoring Comparisons

  • "The sticker price is the price." Sentry's $29/month and Datadog's $15/host/month are entry-level marketing numbers. Actual bills depend on overages, and those overages dominate at even modest scale.

  • "More features = more complexity." Teams assume an all-in-one platform must be harder to operate than a best-of-breed tool. In practice, managing six vendor relationships is far more complex than running one platform.

  • "Self-hosted means self-managed headaches." Modern Docker Compose deployments take 15 minutes. The operational overhead is a few hours per month.

  • "You can't switch off Sentry without a major rewrite." Because Moneat implements the Sentry envelope ingestion protocol, switching is a one-line DSN change per service.

  • "Datadog is expensive, but nothing else covers as much." This was true in 2022. In 2026, platforms like Moneat bundle error tracking, session replays, logs, uptime, status pages, on-call, infrastructure monitoring, and LLM observability — covering more than Datadog's core stack at a fraction of the price.

Practical Playbook: Choosing and Migrating Your Error Monitoring Stack

  1. Do this first: Pull your last 3 months of invoices from your current tool. Calculate your true per-month cost including all overages, add-ons, and per-seat fees.

  2. Model your actual usage across errors/month, replay sessions, log volume (GB), and uptime monitors.

  3. Price out your current stack on all three platforms using the comparison tables above. Include the cost of supplementary tools (PagerDuty, Statuspage.io, etc.).

  4. If you're evaluating self-hosting, estimate your infrastructure cost: a 4-CPU / 8 GB RAM server handles millions of events/day and costs $40-80/month.

  5. Test the migration path before committing. With Moneat, update the DSN in one non-critical service and verify errors, replays, and traces appear correctly.

  6. Avoid this trap: Don't compare only the base plan prices. A $29/month plan with $500 in overages is more expensive than a $79/month plan with generous included limits.

  7. Evaluate data residency requirements early. If you have GDPR, HIPAA, or data sovereignty obligations, self-hosting or a platform with regional hosting options is non-negotiable.

  8. Check SDK compatibility before evaluating any platform. Switching from Sentry to a platform that requires new SDKs is a multi-sprint project. Switching to a Sentry-compatible platform is an afternoon.

  9. Involve your finance team in the evaluation. Observability costs scale with traffic — if your product is growing, your monitoring bill will grow too.

  10. Run a two-week parallel evaluation. Send the same telemetry to your current tool and the new one. Compare the debugging experience, alert quality, and dashboard usability side by side.

Tradeoffs, Caveats, and Second-Order Effects

  • Moneat is newer than Sentry and Datadog. The ecosystem of community plugins, third-party integrations, and StackOverflow answers is smaller.

  • Sentry's profiling is genuinely useful and unique. If continuous profiling is critical to your workflow, Sentry is the only option here.

  • Datadog's enterprise dashboard and correlation capabilities are deep. For organizations with 100+ microservices and dedicated platform engineering teams, Datadog's depth in APM trace analysis may justify the premium.

  • Self-hosting shifts operational responsibility. If your team has zero DevOps capacity, the managed SaaS options eliminate infrastructure maintenance.

  • Pricing models change. All three vendors adjust pricing periodically. The numbers in this post reflect February 2026 published rates.

  • Vendor lock-in is real in both directions. Migrating away from Datadog's proprietary agents is painful. But committing to a self-hosted platform also creates switching costs.

  • AI observability is early everywhere. Moneat's LLM observability is ahead of competitors, but the entire category is nascent. Expect rapid changes from all vendors.
