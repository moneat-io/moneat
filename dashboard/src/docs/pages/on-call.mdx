# On-Call & Incidents

## Overview

Moneat includes a built-in on-call and incident-response system. When critical errors or uptime failures are detected, Moneat can automatically raise on-call alerts and notify the right people through configurable escalation policies. Notifications can be sent via push notifications, Slack DMs, email, SMS, and voice calls.

:::info[Availability]
On-Call is available on SaaS plans that include on-call seats. Self-hosted deployments require a
license key with the `oncall` feature enabled.
:::

## Terminology

Moneat keeps a few closely related concepts distinct so counts, links, and automation stay unambiguous:

- **On-call alert** — a page routed to a responder through an escalation policy. It moves through **triggered → acknowledged → resolved** and is the unit you acknowledge, reassign, and resolve from the mobile app, Slack, or the Alerts view.
- **Native incident** — a declared incident that Moneat coordinates end to end. It carries a severity, an ownership timeline, and its own lifecycle (**triage → active → resolved → post-incident → closed**, with cancelled and declined as terminal states), and it can link one or more on-call alerts. Declared incidents live in the Incidents view.
- **Alert episode** — one source-neutral firing of an alert rule, from first breach through resolution or suppression. Episodes are how repeated notifications for the same condition are grouped.
- **Forwarded provider incident** — an incident that originates in an external incident tool. Moneat delivers and synchronizes it but treats the provider as the system of record.

A single on-call alert can page you without ever becoming a native incident. Declaring an incident is a deliberate step you take when a problem needs coordinated response.

## On-Call Schedules

### Creating a Schedule

On-call schedules define who is responsible for responding to on-call alerts at any given time. Navigate to **On-Call → Schedules** to create and manage schedules.

- Name your schedule (e.g., "Backend Team Primary")
- Add team members as participants
- Configure rotation period (daily, weekly, or custom)
- Set rotation start time and timezone

### Rotations

Rotations automatically cycle through participants on the schedule. When the rotation period ends, the next person in the list becomes the primary on-call responder. You can set up multiple rotation layers for backup coverage.

### Overrides

Need to swap shifts? Overrides let you temporarily assign a different person to be on-call for a specific time period without modifying the underlying rotation schedule.

## Escalation Policies

Escalation policies define what happens when an on-call alert is raised. They consist of ordered steps that are executed in sequence until the alert is acknowledged.

### Priority Levels

| Level | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **P0 - Critical** | Immediate escalation. Production is down. |
| **P1 - High** | Urgent. Major functionality impacted. |
| **P2 - Medium** | Important. Degraded service or workaround needed. |
| **P3 - Low** | Respond within one business day. |
| **P4 - Very Low** | Lower urgency. No page by default. |
| **P5 - Informational** | Tracked for awareness. No page by default. |

### Escalation Steps

Each step specifies who to notify and how long to wait before escalating. For example:

1. Notify the primary on-call via push notification and Slack
2. If not acknowledged within 5 minutes, notify the secondary on-call
3. If still not acknowledged after 10 minutes, notify the engineering manager

### Business Hours

You can configure business hours for your team. Escalation policies can behave differently during and outside of business hours, for example using different timeouts during working hours.

## On-Call Alert Lifecycle

### Alert States

- **Triggered** - On-call alert raised, notifications being sent
- **Acknowledged** - A responder has acknowledged and is investigating
- **Resolved** - The on-call alert has been resolved

### Alert Actions

While an on-call alert is active, responders can:

- **Acknowledge** - Stop escalation and indicate you're working on it
- **Resolve** - Mark the on-call alert as resolved
- **Reassign** - Transfer the on-call alert to another team member
- **Add notes** - Document findings and actions taken

### Timeline

Every on-call alert has an audit timeline showing when it was raised, who was notified, when it was acknowledged, notes added, and when it was resolved.

## Declared Incidents

When a problem needs coordinated response, you declare a native incident. A declared incident adds a severity, responder roles, source links, and a shared, evidence-preserving timeline on top of any on-call alerts it links, and it appears in the Incidents view. See the Terminology section above for how incidents and alerts differ.

### Declaring an incident

There are two ways to open an incident, and both use the same declaration form so your process stays consistent:

- **Standalone** — from **On-Call → Incidents**, choose **Declare incident**. Use this when you are opening an incident directly rather than from a page.
- **From an alert** — on an on-call alert, choose **Declare Incident**. The form is prefilled from the alert, and the alert is linked to the new incident automatically.

Every declaration captures a **title**, an optional **summary** and **description**, a **severity** (SEV-0 through SEV-4), and an **initial status** — **Triage** while you confirm whether it is a real incident, or **Active** once it is confirmed and being worked.

### Modes

The **mode** records what kind of incident this is:

| Mode | Use it for |
|------|-----------|
| **Live** | A real, ongoing incident that needs a response now. |
| **Retrospective** | Recording an incident after the fact, so the history is captured even though the response has ended. |
| **Test** | A drill or game-day exercise. Test incidents are clearly marked so they are never mistaken for real ones. |

### Visibility

Visibility classifies an incident's intended audience:

- **Organization** — classified for an organization-wide audience.
- **Private** — classified as sensitive, intended for responders.
- **Public** — classified as shareable beyond your organization.

Visibility records intent today; enforced private-incident access and a public
share surface are planned rather than active, so treat it as a classification.

### Incident types and forms

Under **On-Call → Configuration** you can tailor what each incident captures:

- **Types** group incidents (for example, *Security* or *Customer-facing outage*) and select which declaration form appears.
- **Custom fields** are reusable inputs — single- and multi-select, text, number, link, and user, team, service, or catalog-resource references.
- **Forms** compose those fields for a stage (Declaration, Acceptance, Update, Resolution, Escalation). For each field you control its **position**, whether it is **visible** or **required**, a **default value**, **help text**, and optional **conditions** that show a field only when another field has a specific value.
- **Roles** define your responder roles (see below).

When you pick a type while declaring, its configured fields render inline and are validated before the incident is created.

:::info[Versioned definitions]
Types, fields, forms, and roles are saved as **versioned snapshots**. Editing a definition creates a new version and supersedes the previous one, and each incident keeps the exact version that was in effect when it was declared — so past incidents never change retroactively.
:::

### Responder roles, participants, and observers

On an incident's **Responders** panel you can see who is doing what and manage membership:

- **Roles** (such as Incident Commander or Communications Lead) can be **claimed** by yourself, **assigned** to a teammate, **unassigned**, or **handed over** to someone else with a handover note.
- **Participants** are actively working the incident; **observers** are following along. You can **join**, **observe**, **leave**, or remove others.

Role assignments and membership changes use optimistic concurrency, so simultaneous edits are detected rather than silently overwriting each other.

:::info[Private responder instructions]
A role can carry **private instructions** for whoever holds it. They are authored under Configuration (and visible there to anyone who can open that page) and delivered privately to the assignee — they are omitted from the general incident detail and timeline.
:::

### Source references

Link the supporting evidence for an incident on its **Sources** panel — a runbook or dashboard **link**, a **Slack message**, or another **message** reference. Each linked source shows its type and opens in a new tab, and you can unlink it at any time. (On-call alerts are attached through the alert linking flow rather than as manual sources.)

### The incident timeline

The incident timeline is the canonical, evidence-preserving record of what happened. Each event shows:

- its **provenance** (where it came from — the dashboard, Slack, an integration, an import, a workflow, or the system),
- its **visibility** (organization, participants, private, or public),
- when it **originally occurred** and when it was **observed**, and
- any **annotation**, whether it was **edited**, and whether it has been **removed**.

The common read path stays calm and scannable. Advanced controls are available per event:

- **Annotate** — add context without changing the original event.
- **Edit** — correct the event type, visibility, time, or details.
- **Reorder** — adjust the order of events when the recorded sequence needs fixing.
- **Remove / Restore** — soft-delete an event and bring it back later. Removed events are hidden by default; turn on **Show removed** to see them.
- **Revision history** — every annotation, edit, reorder, removal, and restore is retained, so the original evidence is never lost.
- **Export JSON** — download the full timeline, including removed events, for the record.

You can filter the timeline by **event type**, **provenance**, and **visibility**.

### Resolving an incident

An active incident can be resolved from its detail page, with an optional resolution note. Adding notes and resolving both record events on the timeline.

## Native incident availability and Alert Routes

Native incident response is available by default to organizations whose plan and license include it. The capabilities endpoint reports entitlement and quota state; there is no organization or deployment rollout switch to enable.

Automatic incident creation is configured separately through ordered **Alert Routes**:

- An enabled route must explicitly turn on **Create an incident for matched alerts**. A route that only pages or groups alerts will not create an incident.
- Routes are evaluated in order. A first matching page-only route prevents later routes from handling the alert, so put incident-capable routes before catch-all paging routes.
- The route editor and test-alert result identify whether no route matched, paging occurred, grouping occurred, an incident was skipped, or a triage incident was created.
- The administrator test-alert response is action-specific: a successful alert request does not imply an incident. Review the route state, matched route revision, group, paging result, and incident result separately.
- The editor includes an opt-in **P0–P2 triage preset**. It pages once per group, creates triage incidents, and derives SEV-0, SEV-1, or SEV-2 from P0, P1, or P2; fixed-severity route settings are unchanged.
- An on-call alert detail shows the matched route revision and independent grouping, paging, and incident outcomes with the reason when an action is skipped or fails.
- Manual **Declare Incident** remains available independently of Alert Route automation.

External incident-provider passthrough is a separate capability and is not affected by native incident entitlement.

## Notification Channels

Moneat supports multiple notification channels for on-call alerts:

- **Push Notifications** - Via the Moneat mobile app (iOS and Android). Responders can acknowledge and resolve from the notification.
- **Slack DMs** - Direct messages with interactive buttons for acknowledge/resolve actions.
- **Email** - Notification emails with on-call alert details and action links.
- **SMS and voice calls** - Twilio-backed fallback channels for urgent escalations.

:::tip[Mobile App]
Install the Moneat mobile app to receive push notifications for on-call alerts. You can acknowledge and resolve on-call alerts directly from your phone.
:::

## Slack Usergroup Sync

You can link an on-call schedule to a Slack usergroup. When the on-call rotation changes, Moneat automatically updates the Slack usergroup membership so that `@on-call` always mentions the current responder.
